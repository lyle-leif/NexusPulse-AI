How it works

From daily numbers to a plain explanation

NexusPulse is a monitoring and root-cause tool for online stores. It answers three questions: did something change, where, and how sure are we?

  1. 01

    Store data

    Visits, orders, refunds and recorded changes (releases, prices, campaigns) are loaded every day. Consistency checks confirm the numbers add up before anything else runs.

    See the data checks

  2. 02

    Expected range

    For each day, a forecast made before that day sets the range revenue, orders, visits and conversion should fall in. Known holidays and closures are left out.

    See revenue vs expected

  3. 03

    Issue detected

    When a number stays outside its range long enough, an issue opens. Tracking problems are told apart from real business changes.

    Replay it day by day

  4. 04

    Explained

    The investigation finds where the change happened (segment), what moved (visits, conversion or order value), and whether a recorded change lines up with it. It says how sure it is.

    See how often it is right

About the data

The demo store is generated, with planted changes whose answers the system cannot see. That is what makes the accuracy page possible. A real public retailer dataset is also tested; every result is labelled Synthetic, Semi-synthetic or Real.

Where AI is used

Numbers, tables and confidence levels never come from a language model. Summaries come from templates; an optional local model may reword them, and its text is checked against the findings and replaced if it fails.

Known limits

Store-wide changes under about 25% are often missed unless they are concentrated in one channel, device, region or category. Small stores are watched store-wide only, and their segments are hard to tell apart. The data is daily, not live.
